📋 What Is Enhanced Pointer Precision?

Enhanced Pointer Precision is a setting built into Windows that adjusts how far your cursor travels based on how quickly you move the mouse. When it is turned on, Windows assumes that a fast flick of the mouse should move the cursor farther than the same physical distance covered slowly. This can feel helpful for general browsing, but many people find it unpredictable during precise tasks such as gaming, photo editing, drawing, or design work.

When it is turned off, the relationship between movement of the physical mouse and movement of the on-screen cursor stays constant. That means the same distance on your mousepad always produces the same distance on the screen. Many users describe this as a more natural or more consistent feel, especially in games where consistent aim matters.

Finding and changing this setting in Windows is not always obvious. Mouse Accel Off exists so that you do not have to search through Control Panel, look for the right pointer options, and remember where the checkbox lives. The utility surfaces the setting and gives you a direct switch.
